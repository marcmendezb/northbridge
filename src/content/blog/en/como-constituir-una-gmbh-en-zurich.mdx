To set up a GmbH in Zurich you need CHF 20,000 of fully paid-up capital, at least one shareholder and one person resident in Switzerland who can represent the company. The process goes through a blocked bank account, the notary and the commercial register. With your paperwork in order, registration usually takes 7 to 10 days.

## What a GmbH is and when it suits you

The GmbH (_Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung_) is the Swiss limited liability company. Its big advantage is that it separates your personal assets from the company's: if something goes wrong, you are liable with the company's capital, not with your home.

Compared with the two other common options:

- **Sole proprietorship (Einzelfirma).** The simplest option, with no capital required, but you are liable with everything you own. It suits you if you work for yourself and the risk is low.
- **Company limited by shares (AG).** Requires CHF 100,000 of capital and involves more formalities. It suits you if you plan to raise large funding rounds or have many shareholders.

The GmbH sits in between: it protects your personal assets, requires less capital than an AG and its shareholders are listed in the commercial register. For most SMEs and many early-stage startups it is the most balanced option.

## The requirements

- **Share capital of CHF 20,000**, fully paid up from day one. It can be contributed in cash or in kind (equipment, for example), but contributions in kind require more documentation.
- **At least one shareholder.** This can be a person or a company. You do not need several.
- **One person resident in Switzerland** to represent the company, as managing director (_Geschäftsführer_) or as a director with signing authority. If all shareholders live abroad, you must appoint someone who lives here.

<Note>
  The capital is not an expense: it still belongs to the company. Once the company is registered,
  the bank releases it and you can use it for the business. What you cannot do is pay it back to
  yourself as a shareholder.
</Note>

## The steps, in order

### 1. Choose the name and check it is available

The name must be clearly distinguishable from existing ones and include the legal form (GmbH). Before anything else, look it up in the central business name index (Zefix) to avoid surprises at the register.

### 2. Draft the articles of association

The articles set out the name, registered office, business purpose and capital. Templates exist, but it is worth adapting them: who decides what, and what happens if a shareholder wants to sell their shares or an investor comes in.

### 3. Open the capital deposit account

You pay the CHF 20,000 into a blocked account in the name of the company in formation. The bank issues a deposit certificate, which the notary needs, and only releases the money once the company is registered.

### 4. Sign at the notary

Forming a GmbH requires a public deed: there is no way around the notary. At the notary's office you sign the articles of association and the deed of formation, which refers to the bank certificate.

### 5. Entry in the commercial register

You or the notary submit the documents to the commercial register of the Canton of Zurich. **The company exists from the moment it is registered**, not from the signing. With complete documents, the Zurich register usually processes the entry in 7 to 10 days; it takes longer near the end of the year, and for the entry to count in the current year the papers must arrive before 1 December.

### 6. Further registrations

- **VAT (MWST):** mandatory once your turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 a year.
- **Social insurance (AHV):** you join a compensation fund as soon as you have employees or pay yourself a salary.
- **Accident insurance:** mandatory for everyone employed in Switzerland.

### 7. Accounting from day one

A GmbH must keep full accounts. Starting in good order from day 1 saves you the most expensive job of all: rebuilding months of transactions at the end of the year.

Since 2021, the federal fee for registering a corporation has been CHF 420. Some websites still mention CHF 600: that is the old rate. The notary charges according to a cantonal tariff; for a simple GmbH, expect the minimum of CHF 500 plus VAT.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing the capital with your personal accounts.** The capital belongs to the company. Paying personal expenses with it, or company expenses from your own account, complicates the accounts and can cause tax problems.
- **Using generic articles of association without reading them.** They work until a new shareholder or investor comes in. Then you find out nobody planned how a share is sold.
- **Forgetting accident insurance.** It is mandatory from your first employee, and not having it can prove costly if there is an accident.
- **Waiting until the end of the year to sort out the paperwork.** The first financial year is the one with the most unusual transactions: capital contributions, formation costs, first invoices.

## Your checklist for setting up a GmbH

- Name checked in Zefix.
- Articles of association adapted to your case.
- A person resident in Switzerland to represent the company.
- CHF 20,000 paid into the capital deposit account and a certificate from the bank.
- An appointment with the notary.
- Entry in the commercial register.
- Registration with the compensation fund and accident insurance, if you have staff.
- VAT registration, if you exceed CHF 100,000 a year.
- Accounts open from day one.
