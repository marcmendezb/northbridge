A Zurich SME whose financial year matches the calendar year has four key dates: VAT every quarter, salaries in January, the company tax return on 30 September and payment of cantonal taxes on the same date. Here is the whole year, month by month, and what we need from you before each date.

## VAT: every quarter, 60 days later

If you use the **effective method**, VAT is reported quarterly. The law sets the deadline at **60 days after the end of each quarter**, both for filing the return and for paying. In practice:

- Q1 (January to March) is due at the end of May.
- Q2 (April to June), at the end of August.
- Q3 (July to September), at the end of November.
- Q4 (October to December), at the end of February or on 1 March, depending on the year.

If you use the **net tax rate** (_Saldosteuersatz_, a fixed percentage based on your activity that simplifies the calculation), you report every six months: the first half is due at the end of August and the second at the end of February.

Since 2025, businesses with turnover of up to about CHF 5 million can apply to **file VAT once a year**. The return is due at the end of February, but with the effective method you have to make payments on account during the year. It is not always worth it: we look at it with you.

### The rates that apply today

The standard rate is **8.1%**, the reduced rate (food, books, medicines) is **2.6%** and the accommodation rate is **3.8%**. We register you once your turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 a year.

<Note>
  On 29 November 2026 there is a vote on a VAT increase to fund the AHV. If it passes, the rates
  would change in 2028 at the earliest. We will let you know well in advance what changes on your
  invoices.
</Note>

## Salaries and social insurance: January

If you have staff, January is the busiest month:

- **30 January:** annual salary declaration to the compensation fund (AHV). If it falls on a weekend or public holiday, the next working day applies. If it arrives late, the fund charges late-payment interest.
- **Salary certificates (Lohnausweis):** every employee needs one for their personal tax return. The law does not set a date, but January or February is reasonable.

During the year, contributions are paid on account every quarter, or every month if the total payroll exceeds CHF 200,000. They are due 10 days after the end of each period.

## Tax returns: March and September

In the Canton of Zurich there are two different dates:

- **31 March:** tax returns for individuals. If you are self-employed, the result of your business goes here.
- **30 September:** tax returns for legal entities (GmbH, AG) for the previous financial year.

If you cannot make it, you can ask for an **extension until 30 November**. You must request it in time; beyond that date it is only granted for exceptional reasons.

### And payment

The cantonal and municipal taxes for the year are also due on **30 September**. If you would rather spread them out, you can pay in instalments during the year. The company's direct federal tax has its own provisional bill, due on 31 March.

<Note>
  Filing the return in September does not mean the tax is paid then for the first time. We look at
  it when we close the year, so you can set money aside every month and September is no surprise.
</Note>

## What we need from you

The rule is simple: **each quarter's documents, before the 15th of the following month.** Invoices issued and received, bank statements and any new contracts. With that, we file VAT on time and your figures stay up to date.

If you work with us, you do not need to remember any of these dates: we write to you before each one with what we need and by when.
